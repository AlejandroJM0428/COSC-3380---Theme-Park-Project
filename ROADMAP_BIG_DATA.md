# Roadmap: an established park's worth of data

Starting point: the Oct 31 project zip (files `SQL/00` to `SQL/16` and `ROADMAP_DEPENDENTS.md`, all stages ticked). Nothing for this roadmap exists yet.

Goal: the demo should look like a park that opened in spring 2025 and has been running ever since, ending on the day the data is loaded. Every report, portal and desk should have a believable amount to show.

Each stage is small and ends with a zip of only the changed files at their repo paths. Tick a stage only when its zip has been delivered. A fresh session resumes from the first unticked stage.

## Decisions (defaults, change only if told)

- **Method.** Extend `SQL/tools/gen_test_data.py`. Do not hand-write INSERTs. The generator is deterministic (seed 3380) and builds every row to obey the triggers, and that must stay true.
- **Priority ranking** (the owner agreed with this focus):
  1. Tickets. They feed Customers per Month and Daily Attendance, and all revenue, capacity and refund figures.
  2. Ride log (boardings and wait readings). It feeds Most Ridden Rides per Month, waits and ride availability.
  3. Maintenance jobs. They feed Maintenance Jobs per Month, repair cost, downtime, lost-revenue estimates and the ride notices.
  4. Weather log. Rain-outs per Month is a required report.
  5. Customers and dependents. The seed has no dependents, so My Family looks empty.
  6. Memberships, perks and cancellation requests. Retention and revenue at risk need history.
  7. Purchases (food and shop). They feed the revenue reports.
  8. Game plays.
  9. Employees, shifts and ratings. They feed Employee Performance, Staff by Zone, Shift Ratings, My Work and the HR desk.
  10. Named staff logins.
- **Build order is not priority order.** Tickets need customers, memberships and weather to exist first, and the ride log needs tickets, weather and maintenance. The stages below follow build order. Each stage says which priority number it serves. If time runs short, protect priorities 1 to 4 and cut from the bottom.
- **Dates.** History starts in spring 2025 (default: 1 April 2025) and ends on the day the generator is run (`date.today()`), not a fixed Oct 22. Memberships already start in January 2025 in the current seed, so the first memberships are moved to match the opening.
- **Est. date.** Add `established: 2025` to `client/src/config/brand.js` and show "Est. 2025" in the header and footer. There is no database column for it and none is added.
- **Volume** (the full-detail version would be hundreds of thousands of tickets, which is too heavy for the demo server and the report pages). Targets, with seasonal peaks:
  - about 25,000 tickets (about 45 a day on average, busiest days several hundred)
  - about 100,000 to 150,000 ride-log rows (4 to 6 boardings per ticket plus wait readings)
  - about 60,000 purchases (the current seed averages 2.5 per ticket)
  - game plays in proportion to tickets, as now
- **Capacity.** `Park.MaxOccupancy` is 120 in `SQL/01_park_and_reference.sql` (`MAX_OCC` in the generator) and the seed already hits it on its sold-out day. The generator raises it. Default 600: the busiest ordinary day stays under about 80% of it and one future demo day is sold out at exactly the limit, so the sold-out and selling-fast notices still show. Stage 1 sets the final number from the measured peak. This is a judgement call, not a rule from the data.
- **Prices.** The seed is generated while the day pass is 79.00 in file 01, and file 15 lowers it to 59.00 afterwards without touching sold tickets. So today's seed tickets are recorded at 79.00 and new ones at 59.00. Default: the generator writes the final price list (59.00, tiers 69 / 89 / 179) into file 01, so history and today agree. Stage 1 confirms this with the owner's report numbers.
- **Staff.** Headcount grows with attendance (default 80 to 100, up from 36), with hire dates spread back to the opening. Made-up names only. Generated weekly hours stay at 40 or under; a few people sit at 36 to 40 and the average stays near 22 to 26.
- **Names.** Made-up names only, everywhere, including dependents.
- **Output.** The generator writes SQL files and a loader runs them. The generator is committed. Its output is not committed if it is large (stage 1 measures it and decides). The loader is a script, because one giant SQL file is awkward to review and slow to load.
- **40-hour rule on actual hours** is an optional last stage (stage 14). Default: not built. The generated data respects 40 either way.

## What the code already does (checked in the repo)

- **Generator.** `SQL/tools/gen_test_data.py` writes `00_clear_data.sql`, `01_park_and_reference.sql` and `02_test_data.sql`. Fixed constants: `FIRST` 2026-07-20, `LAST` 2026-10-21, `TODAY` 2026-10-22, `SOLD_OUT` 2026-11-28, `MAX_OCC` 120. It currently makes 36 employees, 256 customers (6 demo customers with fixed ids 1 to 6), 39 memberships, 16 maintenance jobs, 1,253 tickets, and the weather, refunds, ride log, game plays, purchases and shifts that go with them. All logic lives in one top-to-bottom script, so stages add to it in place.
- **Load order matters.** In `docker-compose.yml`, files 00 (schema), 01 and 02 load first, so while 02 runs only the six INSERT triggers from the schema file are live: `trg_WeatherLog_BI`, `trg_Ticket_BI`, `trg_Membership_BI`, `trg_RideLog_BI`, `trg_GamePlay_BI`, `trg_Purchase_BI`. The 26 later triggers (files 11, 13, 14, 16 and others) do not exist yet. If the loader is run later on a finished database, all 32 fire. That changes results:
  - `trg_Ticket_BI_Guard` (file 14) only checks tickets bought in the last 5 minutes, so back-dated history is not affected.
  - `trg_Ticket_AI_Notify` and `trg_Ticket_AI_SellingFast` only act when the visit date is today or later. Future tickets will create SoldOut and SellingFast notices.
  - `trg_Maint_AI` and `trg_Maint_AU` update `Ride.Status` from the live state, and `trg_Maint_AU_Notify` writes a notice when a job closes with an outcome other than Fixed.
  - `trg_Membership_AI_Welcome` (file 14) grants the welcome perks, so loading memberships late gives the perks for free; loading them early gives none.
  - `trg_Ticket_BI` is redefined in file 16 and prices a dependent's ticket at the discount.
- **Rules every generated row must obey** (from the schema and the triggers):
  - Ticket: visit date on or after the purchase date (`CHK_Ticket_Purchase`); the customer is active; the visit date has fewer unrefunded tickets than `MaxOccupancy`; a membership belongs to the customer, is active on the visit date, and a Basic tier is weekdays only. The price is set by the trigger, whatever the generator writes.
  - Membership: `EndDate` is start + 1 year - 1 day (`CHK_Membership_Term`); no overlap for one customer; the tier is for sale; the price is copied from the tier.
  - Ride log boarding: ticket valid on that date and not refunded, park open, ride active, not under maintenance (`fn_UnderMaintenance`), outdoor rides not during a weather event that closes them, guest height at least the ride minimum, Express only if the ticket has it and the ride allows it. Wait readings have no ticket.
  - Game play and purchase: valid ticket, not under maintenance, item and game active.
  - Maintenance: exactly one target; end date and outcome set together; a closed job needs `RepairCost` (a column added by file 04, which loads after 02); an outcome other than Fixed keeps the facility closed until a later job on it ends as Fixed.
  - Maintenance crew: active technicians only. Ride certification: ride operators only, not before the hire date.
  - Shift: employee active, clock-in not before the hire date, no overlap with the employee's other shifts, clock-out after clock-in, rating 1 to 5 or empty. `Employee.HoursPerWeek` is 40 or less (`CHK_Emp_Hours`). Actual hours worked are not checked anywhere.
  - Refund: not dated before the ticket was bought; a weather refund must point at a weather event that closed rides.
- **Things that are not in the generator today.** `Customer.ParentCustomerID` and `DependentConsent` (file 16), `MemberPerk` (file 14), `MembershipCancellationRequest` (file 16), `Maintenance.RepairCost` and `Shift.ShiftNotes` (file 04). The 24 extra maintenance jobs (ids 17 to 40) are hand-written in `SQL/07_extra_maintenance_data.sql` with fixed July to October 2026 dates, so they would sit in the wrong place in a longer history and must be retired.
- **Logins.** `server/scripts/seed-users.js` makes 8 accounts (one per role). Only `tech` and `cook` are tied to a real `Employee` row, by taking the first active employee with that job title. The other 6 have no employee.
- **Checks that already exist.** `check.sh` (role matrix) and `server/scripts/report-sweep.js` (logs in as every role and hits every report). Both are used in the final stages.
- **List caps.** At least the shift list loads 500 rows (`LIMIT 500` in `server/routes/shifts.js`), and the cancellation page loads 500. With 10 to 20 times more rows some lists will show only the newest part. That is acceptable but must be known, see stage 12.
- **Not found in this zip:** `SYED_BRANCH_NOTES.md`, which the earlier chat mentioned. Stage 11 needs it.

## Deploy order on the server

1. Back up the database.
2. Pull the new code and rebuild the generated files (the loader does this).
3. Apply the history with `apply-history-on-server.sh` (it replaces the existing park data, so the backup matters).
4. Re-run `server/scripts/seed-users.js` so every login is tied to its employee.
5. `deploy-now --allow-infra`.
6. Open the Triggers tab, the five required reports and the home page, and confirm the counts and the notices.
